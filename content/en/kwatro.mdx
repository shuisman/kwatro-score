# Kwatro: rules and scoring

Kwatro is a quick card game for 2 to 4 players, designed by Gene Mackles. You place cards in a grid and score with lines in which everything is either the same or different. Below we explain it in our own words.

## The cards

Every card has three properties: a **colour** (red, blue, green or yellow), a **shape** (circle, square, triangle or cross) and a **number** from 1 to 4. The number is also the card’s point value. Every combination exists exactly once: 4 × 4 × 4 = **64 cards**. There are also **2 wild cards**, worth 0 points.

## Setup

1. Shuffle all cards, including the wild cards.
2. Everyone gets 4 cards and keeps them hidden.
3. The rest is the draw pile. Turn the top card face up in the middle: the grid starts there.
4. The smallest player starts. Play then goes **clockwise**.

## Your turn

You choose one of two things:

- **Play cards.** Place 1 to 4 cards in **a single row or a single column**, connected to the grid. Count your points, then draw back up to 4 cards (as long as the pile allows).
- **Pass.** Optionally put some cards under the pile and take the same number of new ones. You score 0 this turn.

The cards you play in one turn don’t have to touch each other, but they must be in the same line, and no gap may remain in that line.

## The golden rule

A **line** is a row or column of 2, 3 or 4 adjacent cards. Check each property (colour, shape, number): across the whole line it must be **all the same** or **all different**. A line is never longer than 4 cards. A full line of 4 is called a **kwatro**.

If a card you place also forms or extends a line in the other direction, that line must be valid too.

## Scoring

You score right after your turn. This is how:

1. **Base:** look at every line you created or extended this turn. For each line, add up the value of **all** its cards, including the ones that were already there. Then add up all those lines.
2. A card that lies in two lines (a row and a column) counts **twice**.
3. **Doubling:** your whole turn score goes ×2 for
   - every **kwatro** you complete this turn (two of them means ×2 ×2 = ×4);
   - playing **all 4 cards** from your hand;
   - playing your **last card** when the draw pile is empty (this ends the game).

Doublings stack: they apply to the whole turn, not just to the kwatro.

## Worked examples

### A line of three

You place a green square 3 next to a green circle 3 and a green triangle 3.

<Calc>3 + 3 + 3 = **9 points**</Calc>

### One card in two lines

You place a red triangle 3 so that it forms a row with a red circle 1 and a column with a blue triangle 2. You made two lines; the 3 counts in both.

<Calc>(1 + 3) + (2 + 3) = **9 points**</Calc>

### Completing a kwatro

A row holds red 1, blue 2 and green 3, all circles. You add a yellow circle 4: a kwatro worth 10. Below your card there happens to be a yellow cross 4, so you also make a line of two (4 + 4).

<Calc>
  (1 + 2 + 3 + 4) + (4 + 4) = 18

  18 × 2 (kwatro) = **36 points**
</Calc>

### All four cards at once

You place four 4s in a row, each with a different colour and shape. Four cards in one line is always a kwatro, and you play your whole hand.

<Calc>
  4 + 4 + 4 + 4 = 16

  16 × 2 (kwatro) × 2 (all 4 cards) = **64 points**
</Calc>

### The last card

The pile is empty and you have one card left. You use it to make a line of three: 2 + 2 + 2. The game ends immediately.

<Calc>
  2 + 2 + 2 = 6

  6 × 2 (last card) = **12 points**
</Calc>

## Wild cards

- A wild card can stand in for any card and is worth 0 points.
- There must always be one real card that would fit in its place, even if the wild lies in a row and a column.
- A kwatro that contains a wild card still counts as a kwatro and doubles your turn.
- If you hold a card that fits in a wild card’s place, you may swap it before your turn. That doesn’t use your turn and scores nothing.

## End of the game

The game ends **immediately** when the draw pile is empty and someone plays their last card. That turn counts double. The others get no extra turn and their remaining cards don’t count. **The highest total wins.** The rules have no tie-breaker; in Kwatro Score tied players share the win.

## Common mistakes

- **Counting only the new cards.** No: count every card in the line, including the ones already there.
- **Doubling only the kwatro.** No: the whole turn score goes ×2.
- **Placing cards in two rows at once.** All cards of one turn go in one row or column.
- **Making a line of 5.** Not possible: after a kwatro a line is full.
- **Forgetting a crossing line.** Does your card also form a line in the other direction? It scores, and it must be valid too.

## Using Kwatro Score

After each turn, tap in the **total turn score**, including any doubling. The app keeps the standings, shows whose turn it is and who’s next, and creates a result to share at the end.
