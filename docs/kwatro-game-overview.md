# Kwatro (IOTA): Game Overview for a Scorekeeping App

Research date: 2026-10-05. Everything below comes from the official rule sheets (Game Factory *Kwatro* DE/FR, Gamewright *IOTA*, Gene Mackles' original self-published *IOTA* rules and FAQ) and the BoardGameGeek data and forums. Interpretations and inferences are marked as such.

---

## 1. Identity

**Kwatro is the European name for IOTA.** It is the same game with the same 66 cards. It is not *Quarto* (Gigamic's abstract game on a 4x4 board), not the card game *Kwartet*, and not Qwirkle, although it was inspired by Qwirkle and Set.

The project folder is named "kwartro", but the game is spelled **Kwatro**. Its 4-card line is also called a **"kwatro"** (IOTA calls it a **"lot"**). A Dutch-speaking user most likely owns the White Goblin Games edition.

The rules are the same in every edition. The only differences are who goes first and the wording of the kids variant (see sections 3 and 8).

## 2. Components

All editions come in a small metal tin, about 6 x 6 x 3 cm.

- **64 unique number cards.** There is exactly one card for each combination of
  - **Colour (4):** red, blue, green, yellow (yellow is printed orange-ish)
  - **Shape (4):** circle, square, triangle, cross/plus
  - **Number (4):** 1, 2, 3, 4. This is the **face value** and the **points value**. It is printed in the corners and also shown as 1 to 4 small white symbols inside the big coloured shape.
  - 4 x 4 x 4 = 64. No two cards are identical.
- **2 Wild Cards / Jokers**, identical to each other (multicoloured quartered design). **Face value 0.**
- Rule sheet. A pencil and paper are needed for scoring but are *not* included.

Total: **66 cards**.

Discrepancy: White Goblin Games' current shop pages list "72 kaarten" (72 cards). All rulebooks, BGG, reviews and other retailers say 64 + 2 = 66. This is probably a shop-page error, but the user can check their own tin (see Open questions).

Value distribution: there are 16 cards of each value 1, 2, 3 and 4, plus 2 wilds worth 0. The sum of all face values is 16 x (1+2+3+4) = **160**.

## 3. Setup

1. Get pencil and paper (or this app).
2. Shuffle all 66 cards (including the 2 wilds).
3. Deal **4 cards** face down to each player. Players look at their own cards and keep them secret.
4. The rest of the cards form a face-down **draw pile**.
5. Turn the top card of the draw pile face up in the centre. This is the **starter card**.
6. **First player.** The editions differ:
   - Original IOTA (2011): each player draws a random card, the highest face value goes first, and tied players redraw. Then the cards are returned and shuffled.
   - Gamewright IOTA: "The teeniest person starts the game."
   - Game Factory Kwatro: "Der kleinste Spieler beginnt" / "Le plus petit joueur commence" (the smallest player starts).
   - Play then passes **to the left (clockwise)**.

Edge case: if the starter card is a Wild Card, the rules do not say what to do. Players commonly reshuffle and flip again (see Open questions).

## 4. Turn Structure

On your turn, choose **exactly one** of two actions.

### Optional step before your turn: recycle a Wild Card
Before your turn, you may swap a card from your hand for a Wild Card already on the table, if your card is a legal replacement in that position. It must fit every line the wild is part of. You take the wild into your hand.
- Gamewright (by email, quoted on BGG): recycling is **a separate event**. It **does not count as your turn** and **scores no points**. You may play the recovered wild on the same turn or keep it.
- BGG consensus: **any player** may recycle any wild on the table, not only the player who played it.

### Action 1: Play cards
1. Add **1, 2, 3 or 4 cards** from your hand to the grid.
2. All cards played this turn must lie in **one single straight line** (one row *or* one column). At least one must connect to cards already on the grid.
3. The cards do not have to touch each other in that line, and you may add to both ends of an existing line. They must, however, end up as one continuous line with no gaps (see the "no gaps" rule below).
4. Score the turn (section 6) and write it down.
5. Draw back up to **4 cards** (if the draw pile allows).

### Action 2: Pass and trade
Put **some, all or none** of your cards on the **bottom** of the draw pile and draw the same number of new cards. **You score 0** for the turn.

Then play passes to the left.

## 5. Rules: Legal Plays and Edge Cases

### What is a line?
- A **line** is **2, 3 or 4 cards** in a row or column with **no gaps**.
- For **each property separately** (colour, shape, number), the cards in the line must be **all the same or all different**.
  - Example: same colour, different shapes, same number is legal.
  - Example: two 2s and one 4 is **illegal** (numbers are neither all the same nor all different).
- **Any 2 cards always form a valid line.** For each property they are either equal or different. The **first 2 cards** therefore set the pattern, and the 3rd and 4th cards must follow it.
- Because there are only 4 values per property, "all different" across 4 cards uses every value exactly once.
- A line can have the same value in all three properties only if every card is identical, which cannot happen because all 64 cards are unique. In practice, at least one property is always "all different".
- **Maximum line length is 4.** A 4-card line is a **lot** (IOTA) or **kwatro** (Kwatro). Nothing can be added to it.
- **Gaps:** "If there are any gaps, it's not a line... and some spaces on the grid will be impossible to fill." A space whose row and column constraints conflict, or that would make a line longer than 4, or that needs a card already played, is a **dead space**.

### Placement rules
- All cards played on one turn go in **one** straight line. Placing cards in two different rows/columns in one turn is **illegal**.
- As a side effect, a play can **create or extend additional lines** in the crossing direction. Each played card can start or extend one perpendicular line.
- Every line touched must still be legal after the play, in both directions.
- Cards may be placed in any order and on both ends of a line.

### Wild Cards
- A wild substitutes for **any other card** and is worth **0 points**.
- You do not have to name it when you play it. However, it must always be **replaceable by one single real card**. It may *be* one card **or** another, but not one card **and** another.
- If it sits in two lines (a row and a column), it must represent **the same card in both**.
- **Illegal play:** any move after which no single real card could replace the wild. This includes cases where the only possible card has already been played.
- A lot (kwatro) completed with a wild **still counts as a lot** and doubles the turn. The rules do not exclude it, and BGG answers agree. The wild just adds 0 face value.

### Mistakes found later (from the original IOTA FAQ)
If an earlier illegal play is discovered, you may:
- leave it (that line can then never become a lot), or
- restart, or
- treat the bad card as replaceable like a wild, before your turn, without using your turn.

The rules do not say to take back points that were already scored.

### Passing and the draw pile
- When the draw pile is empty, you can no longer refill your hand, so hands shrink.
- Passing when the draw pile is empty has no effect: you cannot trade.
- The rules do **not** cover the case where nobody can play (see Open questions).

## 6. Scoring (exhaustive)

### 6.1 When scoring happens
- **Only immediately after each turn** in which cards were played.
- A **pass scores 0**.
- There is **no end-of-round scoring**, **no end-of-game bonus for remaining hands**, and **no penalties** of any kind.
- Recycling a wild scores **0**.
- The Kwatro French rules say "après chaque manche" (after each round), but this means each turn.

### 6.2 Base points (face value)
For **every line that was created or extended** on this turn:
- add up the **face values of ALL cards in that line**, including cards that were already on the table (1 to 4 each, wild = 0).

Then **sum** over all those lines.
- A card that belongs to **two** scored lines (a row and a column) is **counted twice**, once per line.
- A line that the play did not touch is **not** scored.
- A single card placed next to one existing card creates a 2-card line, and **both** cards count.

So:

```
base = Σ over lines L created or extended this turn ( Σ face values of all cards in L )
```

Line value ranges:

| Line length | Min face sum | Max face sum |
|---|---|---|
| 2 cards | 0 (wild + wild) | 8 (4 + 4) |
| 3 cards | 1 (wild + wild + 1, theoretical) | 12 (4 + 4 + 4) |
| 4 cards (lot) | 0 (theoretical) | **16** (4 + 4 + 4 + 4, same number) |

A lot with **all-different numbers** is always worth exactly **10** (1+2+3+4), or less if a wild stands in for a number. A lot with **all-same numbers** is worth 4n (4, 8, 12 or 16), or 3n if one card is a wild.

### 6.3 Multipliers (all are doublings and they stack multiplicatively)

| Condition | Effect |
|---|---|
| Each **lot / kwatro completed** this turn (any 4-card line created or finished by this play, in either direction) | ×2 per lot |
| Playing **all 4 cards** from your hand in one turn | ×2 |
| **Game-ending play:** the draw pile is empty and you play your **last card** | ×2 |

The doubling applies to **the entire turn's points**, not only to the lot's own points. All three sources (Gene Mackles, Gamewright, Game Factory) agree: "Double points for the entire turn for each lot completed. Double again for playing all 4 of your cards." The Kwatro example also shows the whole subtotal being doubled. A 2025 BGG post suggested doubling only the lot's points, but that contradicts the official example.

```
turn score = base × 2^(lots completed) × (2 if all 4 hand cards played) × (2 if game-ending last card)
```

Useful consequences for the app (inferred from the rules):
- **Playing 4 cards always means at least 1 lot.** The 4 cards must lie in one line, and the maximum line length is 4. So a 4-card play is always a complete 4-card line made of your own cards, and the multiplier is **at least ×4**.
- **Number of lots per turn.** The maximum is **5**: the main line plus one perpendicular lot through each of the 4 played cards. In practice it is 0 to 2, and 3 is rare.
- **Theoretical maximum multiplier:** 2^5 (lots) × 2 (all 4) × 2 (last card) = **×128**.
- **Theoretical maximum base:** 5 lines × 16 = 80. This makes the theoretical maximum turn **10,240**, but that is practically unreachable because of card uniqueness and line constraints.
- The multiplier is **always a power of two**: 1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32, 64 or 128.

### 6.4 Worked examples

**A. Official play example (identical in IOTA and Kwatro, values confirmed from the Kwatro DE sheet)**

| Turn | What happened | Calculation | Score |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Starter red triangle 2. Player adds blue cross 2 and green circle 2 to make a row (same number, different colours, different shapes). | 2 + 2 + 2 | **6** |
| 2 | 2 cards (red circle 3 above the green circle 2, yellow circle 1 below it) make a new 3-card column (same shape, different colours, different numbers). The cards do not touch each other. | 3 + 2 + 1 | **6** |
| 3 | 2 cards. The yellow square 2 completes a **kwatro** (row 2+2+2+2), and 2 new 2-card lines also form. | lot 8 + line 5 (2+3) + line 4 (1+3) = 17; ×2 for one lot | **34** |
| 4 | **4 cards** played. They complete **2 kwatros** and extend 1 line. | lot 10 (3+2+1+4) + lot 10 (2+3+4+1) + line 6 (3+2+1) = 26; ×2 (lot 1) = 52; ×2 (lot 2) = 104; ×2 (all 4 cards) = 208 | **208** |

Running total after 4 turns: 6 + 6 + 34 + 208 = **254**.

**B. Original IOTA FAQ example.** Two cards are added at a corner of the starter so that each forms a 2-card line with it, and the blue square 4 lies in both lines. The total is **13**: the 4 is counted twice. Adding a 3rd card (green circle 1) to one of the lines raises the total to **14**.

**C. Kwatro classroom article (Volgens Bartjens, 2012/13).**
- A green plus 4 placed above a green circle 4 makes a 3-card line of three 4s: 4 + 4 + 4 = **12**.
- Placed elsewhere so that it sits in two 2-card lines: (2 + 4) + (3 + 4) = **13**.
- A best play of three cards that completes a 4-card line: base 30, ×2 = **60**.

**D. Constructed example with the game end.** The draw pile is empty. The player plays their last 2 cards, which complete one lot worth 9 and also make a 2-card line worth 5.
Base 14, ×2 (lot) = 28, ×2 (last card, game end) = **56**. The all-4 bonus does not apply because only 2 cards were played.

**E. Constructed example with a wild.** A wild completes a lot (3 + 3 + 3 + wild) = 9, and no other line is touched. Base 9, ×2 = **18**.

## 7. Game End and Winner

- **End condition:** the game ends **when the draw pile is empty and one player plays the last card in their hand**.
- That player's points **for that turn are doubled** (in addition to any other doublings).
- The game ends **immediately**. Other players do **not** get a final turn. Their remaining hand cards carry **no penalty** and give **no bonus** to anyone.
- **Winner:** the player with the **highest total score**.
- **Tie-breaker:** **none** in any official rule set (see Open questions).
- **Rounds:** none. The game is one continuous sequence of turns, and the number of turns per player is not fixed because of passing.
- A Dutch classroom article describes the bonus as going to "the player who *first* plays their last card". This means the same thing, since the game ends on that play.

## 8. Official Variants and Common House Rules

### Official
| Variant | Source | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| **Short game** | Gamewright, Kwatro DE/FR | Use **half the deck**, including the wild cards. |
| **Young children** | Gamewright | **Ignore scoring.** The first player out of cards after the draw pile runs out wins. |
| **Young children** | Kwatro DE/FR | **Skip scoring** entirely and focus on learning the logic. No winner rule is given. |
| **Basic scoring** | Original IOTA FAQ (Mackles) | **1 point per card** in each created or extended line (cards in 2 lines count twice). Double for each lot, double for playing all 4 cards, and double for the first player to play their last card. |
| **Minimal scoring** | Original IOTA FAQ | Only **1 point** each for completing a lot, playing all 4 cards, and/or being first to play your last card. |
| **Kids arithmetic** | Volgens Bartjens (Dutch teachers' article) | 1 point per counted card, and/or only 1 card per turn. |

### House rules from BGG
- **Additive multipliers:** each bonus adds +1 to the multiplier instead of doubling. 1 lot gives ×2, 2 lots or lot + all-4 give ×3, 2 lots + all-4 give ×4 (instead of ×8). This reduces swinginess.
- **Lot-only doubling:** double only that lot's own points, while all-4 still doubles the whole turn.
- **Remove the wild cards** (especially for 2 players).
- **"Qwirkle-style":** no wilds, 1 point per tile, +4 per completed lot, +4 game-end bonus. Mulligan if you have no legal move. Everyone gets the **same number of turns**. Use markers on dead spaces.
- **Solo:** after each turn, discard the top card of the draw pile face down; play until the deck is gone. Reported solo scores are about **200 to 330**.
- **Scoreless race:** split the deck between players. Discard 1 card from your own deck per existing card your play touched. Completing a lot gives an extra turn. The first to empty their hand wins.
- **Compact table:** overlap the white card borders.
- **Turn timer** against analysis paralysis (Geeky Hobbies review).
- **SET-like:** 8 cards face up, find a 4-card "kwatro" (each property all-same, all-different or two pairs).

## 9. Implications for a Scorekeeping App

### 9.1 Structure
- **Game** → **players** (2 to 4, a single player for solo) → **turns** in seat order.
- **No rounds** in the rules. A "round" (each player takes one turn) is fine as a **display grouping** (table rows), but the last round is usually **incomplete** because the game ends on the turn of the player who goes out.
- Seat order and starting player: store them. The app might suggest the edition's rule ("youngest/smallest player starts").
- Optional game settings: rule set (standard / basic 1-point / additive multipliers / lot-only doubling / no scoring), half deck (yes/no), wilds in (yes/no), equal-turns house rule (yes/no).

### 9.2 Per-turn inputs
Offer two input modes.

**Quick mode** (what most people do on paper):
1. **Base points.** Keypad. Typical 2 to 30, max 80.
2. **Lots completed.** Tap 0, 1, 2, 3 (4 or 5 behind "more").
3. **All 4 cards played?** Toggle.
4. **Game-ending last card?** Toggle. This also ends the game.
5. **Pass.** One button that records 0.

The app calculates: multiplier = 2^lots × (2 if all-4) × (2 if last card), and turn score = base × multiplier. Show the arithmetic in the same way as the rulebook (for example "26 → 52 → 104 → 208").

**Detailed mode** (teaches scoring and prevents mistakes):
- For each line scored this turn, enter its card values by tapping 0 (wild), 1, 2, 3 or 4, one tap per card in the line (2 to 4 taps).
- The app sums each line, marks a line with 4 cards as a **lot** automatically, sums the lines, then asks about all-4 and last card.
- Optional: the app infers "all 4 played" from the number of cards played if that is also entered.

### 9.3 Per-game state to track
- Running total per player, plus the turn log (base, lots, all-4, last-card, multiplier, score, pass flag) for undo/edit.
- **Draw-pile empty** flag. The user sets it once the last card is drawn. Only then is the "last card / game end" option enabled.
- The game-ending player and the final ranking. Highlight ties.
- Optional statistics: highest turn, number of lots, number of passes, biggest multiplier.

### 9.4 Validation and preventable mistakes
| Mistake | App safeguard |
|---|---|
| Doubling only the lot's points instead of the whole turn | Always multiply the full base, and show the calculation. |
| Adding the bonuses (+2x) instead of multiplying (x2, x2, x2) | Multiplier is shown as a power of two. |
| Forgetting that all-4 implies at least one lot | If "all 4 played" is on and lots = 0, warn and suggest lots = 1. Effective minimum ×4. |
| Counting a crossing card only once | In detailed mode each line is entered separately, so a shared card naturally appears twice. Add a hint text. |
| Counting only the newly played cards instead of all cards in the line | Hint text: "count every card in the line, old and new". |
| Wild valued as non-zero | 0-key labelled "Wild". |
| Line value impossible (> 16, or a 4-card line with numbers that are neither all-same nor all-different) | Detailed mode: reject a 4-card line whose numbers are not all equal or a permutation of 1-2-3-4 (wilds excepted). Reject 3-card lines with exactly two equal numbers. |
| Base > 80, lots > 5, lots greater than the number of lines entered | Soft warnings. |
| Applying the last-card double while the draw pile still has cards, or twice in a game | Enable only after "draw pile empty", once per game, then end the game. |
| Scoring a pass or a wild recycle | Pass button = 0. Recycling is not a scoring event. |
| Turn order slip | Auto-advance to the next player, with undo. |

### 9.5 Typical values (for keypad and quick picks)
- **Common turn base values:** about 3 to 25. Single cards next to low cards score 2 to 8. Good multi-line plays score 15 to 30.
- **Frequent quick picks for base:** lot values **8, 10, 12, 16**, and common totals such as 6, 9, 13, 17.
- **Turn scores** after multipliers: usually under 40. Lot turns are typically **20 to 70**. Big turns of **100 to 250+** happen (the official example is 208). Very rare turns reach the high hundreds or more. The total entry field needs **at least 4 digits**, and 5 to be safe, since the theoretical maximum is 10,240.
- **Final totals:** no published distribution exists. Solo fan-variant totals are about 200 to 330. Reviewers describe swings "from six to six hundred" in a single turn. Expect multiplayer winners in the low-to-mid hundreds with large spread (this is an estimate, not sourced). Design for up to 5-digit totals.
- **Number of turns:** with 66 cards, 4 per player in hand and 1 starter, and all players taking turns until the pile is exhausted, a game typically has about 10 to 25 turns per player, plus passes. This is an estimate based on 1 to 4 cards per play.

### 9.6 Nice-to-have
- A rules cheat-sheet, including the "two cards set the pattern" check (for each of colour, shape and number: all same or all different?).
- Rule-set presets for the official variants and the BGG house rules (additive multiplier, lot-only doubling, 1 point per card).
- Optional tie handling: shared win by default, with configurable house tie-breakers.

## 10. Open Questions / Ambiguities

1. **"All 4 cards" bonus late in the game.** When the draw pile is empty and a player holds fewer than 4 cards, does playing their whole (smaller) hand count as "all 4"? The rules literally say "all 4 of your cards", so the strict reading is **no**. The game-end ×2 covers going out. Decide on this.
2. **Tie-breaker.** None exists officially. Use a shared victory or a house rule (for example the player who went out, or the most lots).
3. **Stalemate.** If the draw pile is empty and nobody can (or will) play, the rules give no ending. Suggested house rule: the game ends when all players pass in succession, without the last-card bonus.
4. **Equal number of turns.** By the official rules the game ends immediately. Some groups let everyone finish the round, so the app should make this a setting.
5. **Wild card as the starter card.** Not covered. Most groups reshuffle it into the deck.
6. **Lots completed with a wild.** Officially not excluded, so they count. Some groups disallow it or remove the wilds.
7. **Who may recycle a wild, and when.** Before your own turn, and by any player according to consensus and the Gamewright email. It is not a turn and scores 0. The Kwatro rules only say "you may swap a joker if you have a card in hand that fits".
8. **Card count in the user's tin.** White Goblin's shop says **72 cards**, while every rulebook says 64 + 2. Check the actual box.
9. **Which edition and language** the user owns (White Goblin NL, Game Factory DE/FR, Gamewright EN). This affects terms in the UI: "kwatro" or "lot", "joker" or "wild card".
10. **Kids variant winner.** Gamewright says the first player out wins. Kwatro just says play without scoring. Should the app support a "no scoring, first out wins" mode?
11. **Which house scoring rules** (if any) the user's group uses (additive multipliers, lot-only doubling, 1 point per card).
